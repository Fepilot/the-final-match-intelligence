How THE FINAL works

This is an interactive data story built entirely from match event data — every coordinate is a recorded action (a pass, a tackle, a shot), never a continuous player position. Nothing is drawn between two events.

Explore the match

  • Press Play to replay the match, or drag the timeline to scrub to any moment.
  • Adjust speed, or narrow the view with the period, team, player and event type filters.
  • Persist marks keeps every event visible for the selected period instead of fading it after ~45s.
  • Normalise mirrors both teams onto one shared attacking direction for easier comparison.
  • Goals, cards and big chances are marked directly on the density timeline below the pitch.

What the data tells us

8 editorial story moments. Click one and its supporting evidence loads straight onto the pitch and timeline — insight first, then the events that back it up.

Player explorer

Pick any of the 34 players to isolate their events: passes, shots, recoveries, defensive actions and zone counts. This is an event footprint, deliberately not a heat map — it counts recorded actions, not tracked movement.

Spain v Argentina

Head-to-head metrics and territory by period, plus the full shot map. A separate second source panel holds report-only figures (pressures, line breaks, recovery time) that the event feed itself can't produce.

About the data

Sources, every correction applied to the raw file, the exact rule behind every derived metric, and what's deliberately left blank (no xG, no possession %, no physical data, no formation shape).

Metrics the event feed cannot produce

Source · post-match report
The official report measures things the event file never records — pressures, line breaks, recovery time, phases of play. None of these figures can be recomputed from the events, so they are shown separately and never mixed into the counts above. Read together they say the same thing twice: Argentina worked harder off the ball and got the ball back slower.
Argentina applied 48% more pressures than Spain and still needed 22.3 seconds to win the ball back against Spain's 10.2. They spent a third of the final in a low block. Spain recorded 251 receptions in the final third; Argentina 52.
